Solar atmospheric water generator (AWG)

Status: prototype design stage. We are building and testing our prototype.

The problem

Safe water is a basic human need, but for billions of people it is still out of reach. According to the World Health Organization (data from 2022), 2.2 billion people lacked safely managed drinking water. Rural communities are hit hardest.

The idea

The air around us holds water. Even in dry regions, there is water vapor in the air. An atmospheric water generator (AWG) pulls that water out of the air. We are developing a low-cost atmospheric water generator powered by the sun, with no grid power needed and little maintenance.

How it works

  1. At night, it soaks up water. Night air is cool and humid. Slow fans push outside air through a bed of desiccant, a material such as silica gel that pulls water vapor out of the air and holds it.
  2. By day, the sun releases it. The desiccant sits in a glass-topped, insulated box. The sun heats it, and the hot desiccant gives its water back as vapor.
  3. The vapor turns back into water. The hot, humid air goes round a closed loop through a shaded box with plain aluminum walls. The vapor condenses on the cooler metal, runs down into a drip channel and drains into a sealed container.

Nothing moves except air, a few light flaps and slow fans. A small, low-cost controller reads temperature and humidity sensors and runs the daily schedule.

The prototype

  • Built with simple woodworking: plywood and lumber, glued and screwed. Metal parts are bought ready-made or cut with tin snips.
  • Two desiccant beds and one shared condenser.
  • Sensors measure temperature, humidity and the weight of water collected, so every test can be measured and compared.
  • The design comes with a build guide, controller software and a test plan.

Not yet for drinking. Water from the prototype should be tested by a certified lab or a local health authority before anyone drinks it.
